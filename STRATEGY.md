# HerbalBath — strategy review and 12-month roadmap

A second pass, deeper than the channel list. The channel advice in `GROWTH.md`
still holds, but it was answering "where do I find more customers." That's the
wrong first question. This document answers the right ones: *why does this
business have a low ceiling regardless of channel*, and *what changes the
ceiling itself*.

---

## Part 0 — What I under-weighted the first time

Three things I should have caught and didn't.

**1. Your best-selling offer is a churn machine.** "买3送1" sells 4 bottles for
S$117. Four bottles at 3–4 months each is **12–16 months of supply**. You are
paying 25% of your margin to make your customer disappear for over a year, and
during that year they forget your name, your number, and the habit. A
single-bottle buyer who reorders on time is worth ~S$156/year. Your best
customers are worth less than your worst ones. Fix in Part 1.

**2. Stock in the cupboard is your competitor.** Four bottles at home means
zero urgency, zero attention, and a real chance two of them get given away or
expire. Physical inventory in a customer's home suppresses their next purchase
more effectively than any rival brand does.

**3. The missing small SKU is why every community channel looked expensive.**
I recommended goodie-bag sponsorship at clan associations and temples without
noticing you have nothing to put in a goodie bag. 300 bags × a S$39 bottle is
a S$2,400–3,600 experiment. That's why those channels felt out of reach — not
because they're wrong, but because you lack the unit that makes them cheap.

---

## Part 1 — Five structural moves

These change the economics. Channels only multiply whatever the economics
already are, so these come first.

### 1.1 Convert the bundle to prepaid quarterly delivery

Keep the price. Keep the "4 bottles for the price of 3" framing. Change only
**when the bottles arrive**.

> 买3送1 · S$117 · 我们每3个月送一瓶到您家，用完刚刚好

Same cash upfront. Same perceived deal. What you gain:

- **Four delivery touchpoints a year instead of one.** Each is a conversation,
  a chance to hear how they're doing, and a natural referral moment.
- **No forgotten cupboard stock**, so urgency and attention stay alive.
- **A predictable fulfilment schedule** — you know in advance what ships each
  month, which is the difference between your mother packing calmly and
  packing in a panic.
- **A renewal conversation at month 12** instead of silence.

Some customers will want all four immediately. Let them — don't fight it. Frame
the default as 免费保管 + 每次免运费 and most will take it, because for this
demographic "you'll remember for me" is a feature, not a restriction.

This costs nothing to implement and is probably worth more than any new channel
in this document.

### 1.2 Sell a 90-day protocol, not a bottle

Right now you sell an object. Reframe to **关节保养90天计划** — the bottle plus
a structured programme delivered over WhatsApp.

| Day | Message | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Welcome + a Chinese voice note on correct technique | Most failures are under-use, not non-efficacy |
| 3 | "开始用了吗？" | Catches the ones who never opened the box |
| 7 | First check-in | Early habit formation |
| 21 | "多数人这时候开始有感觉" + ask for a 1–5 rating | **This is your testimonial engine, running itself** |
| 45 | Mid-point + simple joint exercises | Value beyond the product |
| 75 | Running low | Reorder, already built |
| 90 | Graduation + referral ask | Ask at peak satisfaction, not at random |

Why this is the highest-leverage idea here:

- **Adherence drives results, results drive retention and referral.** A spray
  used twice daily for 90 days works far better than one used sporadically for
  a fortnight, and nothing in your current funnel influences that.
- **The day-21 rating is an automated NPS and testimonial pipeline.** You
  currently source testimonials manually. This sources them continuously, at
  the moment of peak novelty, with the customer's own words.
- **It converts a commodity into a service.** Every shophouse liniment is a
  bottle. None of them is a programme.
- **It makes community partnerships natural.** You cannot offer an Active
  Ageing Centre "a spray." You can offer them "a 90-day joint care programme
  for your seniors." Same product, entirely different conversation, and the
  second one gets past the gatekeeper.
- **It's ~7 messages at ~S$0.02 each — about 14 cents per customer.**

And it is precisely the thing your background makes trivial and your
competitors will never build: a state machine over WhatsApp.

### 1.3 Introduce a 20–30ml 随身装 at ~S$16

The single most unlocking SKU decision available to you. One small bottle is
simultaneously:

- **The goodie-bag unit** — turns a S$3,000 clan association experiment into a
  S$600 one, which makes the entire community channel affordable
- **The clinic tester** — what the sinseh applies during a session
- **The impulse price point** — S$16 is "try it," S$39 is "commit"
- **The handbag and tour unit** — used at the hawker centre, at church, on a
  10-day coach tour, which is also where it gets *seen* by peers
- **The gift add-on** that makes a gift box look generous

Size it at 20–30ml deliberately: roughly two weeks of use on one joint. Enough
to feel the effect and start a habit. A 5ml sachet gets used twice and
forgotten — that's sampling theatre, not trial.

**This has an 8–12 week lead time and it gates the entire Q4 calendar.**
See Part 3.

### 1.4 Price *up* to the adult child, not down to the senior

The same product has completely different price elasticity depending on who
pays. A Chinese Singaporean senior calls S$39 for herself 浪费钱. Her daughter
calls S$88 for her mother *cheap*, because the daughter isn't buying a spray —
she's buying relief from the guilt of not visiting enough, and a solution to
"what do I even get her."

So build a **孝心礼盒 at S$88**: two full bottles, one 随身装, a proper box, and
a handwritten Chinese card carrying the child's own message.

The detail that makes it work: **send the child a photo when it's delivered.**
That closes the emotional loop the child actually bought. It costs you one
photo and it is the entire product.

Note what this inverts: everyone in this category discounts toward the senior.
You premium-price toward the child, through completely different channels
(Xiaohongshu, Facebook, workplace gifting, insurance agents), for the same
manufacturing cost.

### 1.5 Treat HSA listing as a moat, not a compliance cost

Your instinct as an engineer will be to file this under bureaucratic overhead.
It's the highest-defensibility investment on this list.

Most of your competitors are shophouse brands with no product listing, no GMP
documentation, no barcode, no dossier. Proper classification and listing is the
*only* key that opens: pharmacy chains, institutional and eldercare buyers,
corporate gifting procurement, nursing homes, and any eventual export.

And HSA has been moving toward mandatory listing for this category. When that
lands, a chunk of your informal competition is removed from the shelf by
regulation while you are already through the door. Very few moats in a
commodity category are available to a small operator. This one is, it's
purchasable, and it takes months — which is exactly why it starts in week 1.

## Part 2 — Four channel insights the first pass missed

### 2.1 The domestic helper is the actual user for your best segment

For customers over ~75 — your highest-need, highest-frequency segment —
somebody else is usually applying the spray. In Singapore that is very often a
migrant domestic worker. She applies it, she notices when it's empty, she tells
the employer to buy more, and her technique determines whether it works at all.

Nobody in this category markets to her. Practical implications:

- **Put application instructions in English, Bahasa Indonesia and Tagalog** in
  the box. This is pure product improvement, costs one insert, and directly
  raises efficacy for your most valuable users.
- Helper communities are dense and highly connected — Sunday gatherings,
  Lucky Plaza, Peninsula Plaza, City Plaza, and very large Facebook groups.
  Word travels faster there than in any channel you're currently using.

One caution, and it matters: keep helpers on **exactly the same captain terms
as anyone else** — a normal referral reward for a genuine referral. Do not
build a special scheme that incentivises them to steer an employer's spending;
some employment terms restrict outside income, and the reputational downside is
asymmetric. The multilingual insert is where the real, clean leverage is.

### 2.2 Hospital discharge is the highest-intent moment in the market

Every day, older Singaporeans go home from SGH, TTSH, CGH, KTPH and NUH after
falls, hip and knee replacements. A frightened family member is buying
everything they might possibly need, all at once, with no price sensitivity.

The channel that sits exactly at that moment is **medical equipment rental
shops** — wheelchairs, hospital beds, commodes, walking frames. They are small
owner-operated businesses, they want add-on margin, and their customer is a
caregiver with a wallet already open. Also: home care agencies and home-visit
physiotherapy providers.

Higher intent than any clinic, and essentially uncontested.

### 2.3 Chinese-language search in Singapore is nearly uncontested

Your RAG pipeline generates English and bilingual content, competing against
the entire global anglophone health internet. Meanwhile "膝盖痛 新加坡" and
"关节痛 怎么办" have almost no serious competition and are what your buyers
actually type. Same pipeline, repoint the topic list. This is the cheapest SEO
arbitrage you have.

### 2.4 Ask your existing customers who referred them

You have been running a referral programme for eight years without knowing it —
your own site says customers recommend you to 亲朋好友. That network already
exists; you simply have no map of it.

One broadcast to your base — "您是听谁介绍的？" with a small credit for
answering — reveals the topology. Some of those 15,000 have personally referred
ten or twenty people. **Those are your captains, and you already know them.**
Recruiting strangers into a referral programme is hard; promoting people who
have already been doing the job for free is not.

## Part 3 — The roadmap

Sequenced by dependency and by the calendar, which for a senior-focused
Singaporean product is unusually decisive.

### The three dates everything hangs off

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **25 Sep 2026** | 中秋节 Mid-Autumn | Clan associations, CCs and temples run events with gift bags |
| **18 Oct 2026** | **重阳节 / Seniors' Day** — a **Sunday** | The single best day in the year for this product. Literally the festival for respecting the elderly. Every clan association, temple, CC and AAC runs a 敬老 event |
| **6 Feb 2027** | 农历新年 CNY | Peak gifting; the 孝心礼盒 window |

*(Lunar dates — worth confirming against a Singapore almanac before committing
spend.)*

**The consequence: goodie-bag and sponsorship decisions for 重阳 get made 4–8
weeks ahead, which means you are pitching in early September, which means the
随身装 order must be placed within about two weeks to have stock.** That single
dependency drives the whole of Phase 0.

### Phase 0 — Weeks 1–4 (August): unblock the long lead times

Do only the things whose clock is already running.

1. **Order the 20–30ml 随身装.** 8–12 week lead time. Everything in Q4 depends
   on it. This is the most time-critical item in this document.
2. **Start HSA classification and listing.** Months, not weeks. Starting it in
   November means missing the whole year.
3. **Connect Redis, import the customer base, run the reorder cron in
   dry-run.** Already built (`GROWTH.md`). The import is an afternoon.
4. **Switch the bundle to prepaid quarterly delivery.** Copy change, no code.
5. **Send the "谁介绍您的？" broadcast** and map the referral graph.
6. **Get a 3PL quote** so you know your threshold before volume arrives.

### Phase 1 — Weeks 5–12 (Sept–mid Oct): prove one channel, land 重阳

1. **Ship the 90-day protocol.** Seven templates and a cron. A weekend.
2. **Practitioner channel: 20 doors.** TCM clinics, medical halls, tuina and
   reflexology. Measure units per door per month; kill anything under 8.
3. **Pitch 重阳 sponsorships in early September** — three clan associations, two
   Mandarin church senior fellowships, two temples. 随身装 in the bags.
4. **Recruit the first 10 captains** from the referral graph you mapped, not
   from strangers.
5. **Pitch 8world** with the founder story. Print desks pick up what performs.

**Gate before Phase 2:** at least 10 doors clearing 8 units/month, and a
measurable reorder rate from the day-75 nudge. If neither is true, the problem
is the offer, not the channel count — fix it before multiplying.

### Phase 2 — Weeks 13–24 (mid Oct 2026–Jan 2027): multiply, and build for CNY

1. **Scale to 50–80 practitioner doors.** This is the grind, and it's
   front-loaded rather than recurring.
2. **Launch the 孝心礼盒 by early December** — CNY gifting decisions start in
   December for a February new year.
3. **Xiaohongshu and Facebook aimed at the daughter**, not the mother. KOC
   posts, not polished KOL.
4. **Two AAC operator HQs** with the 90-day programme framing.
5. **Medical equipment rental shops and home care agencies** — the discharge
   channel.
6. **Insurance agencies for CNY client gifting** — one MDRT team is 200 agents
   with ~50 older clients each.
7. **Shin Min pitch**, carrying whatever 8world coverage you got.

### Phase 3 — Months 7–12 (Feb–Aug 2027): compound

1. **Trade dossier plus real velocity data → pharmacy conversations.** Only now.
   Walking into Guardian without velocity numbers is how you get bad terms.
2. **Corporate and institutional gifting** — town council sponsors, banks,
   union welfare, nursing homes.
3. **Multilingual inserts and the helper channel.**
4. **Consider Malaysia** — only if Singapore doors are stable and fulfilment
   has moved off your kitchen table.

## Part 4 — What not to do

- **No paid social prospecting to 55+.** The arithmetic at S$39 AOV doesn't
  work and it doesn't compound. Every dollar there is a dollar not spent
  recruiting an intermediary who sells for you indefinitely.
- **No pharmacy chains until Phase 3.** Slow, margin-destroying, and they will
  ask for velocity data you don't have. Get it from clinics first.
- **No app. No marketplace. No custom checkout.** Your CS background is a trap
  in this business — the temptation is to build software when the constraint is
  distribution and compliance. The only software worth writing is the three
  narrow things already built: reorder, attribution, restock. Everything else
  is procrastination that feels like progress.
- **No rebrand**, even though 草药浴 ("herbal bath") is genuinely a poor name
  for a spray and does cost you some word-of-mouth clarity. Eight years of
  equity outweighs it. Revisit only if you ever launch a second line.
- **No serious TikTok Shop push.** Wrong demographic, high ops load.

## Part 5 — Two facts that would change this plan

I've written the above without knowing these, and both fork the roadmap. Worth
establishing in week 1.

**1. Is "15,000 customers" cumulative over 8 years, or currently active?**
Cumulative at ~1,900/year is a very different business from 15,000 live
relationships. If it's cumulative, Phase 0 is a *reactivation* campaign and the
business could plausibly double in 90 days without a single new channel. If
it's active, Phase 1 matters much more.

> **Answered (8 Aug 2026):** "15,000" is a rough lifetime estimate, not a
> tracked figure. Actual current pace is ~15–20 sales/day (~450–600/month).
> That rules out the optimistic fork above — there is no large dormant base to
> blast a reactivation message at. Phase 1's channel work matters more than
> Part 3 assumed. See `/internal/roadmap` for the corrected plan.

**2. Do you have contactable phone numbers for them?** Everything in
`GROWTH.md` and section 1.2 assumes yes. If those 15,000 exist only as
marketplace order records with no reachable contact, then reconstructing that
list — even partially, even approximately — is the highest-value work available
to you, ahead of every channel here.

> **Answered (8 Aug 2026):** No CRM — reorders arrive as individual inbound
> WhatsApp chats/calls. Roughly 200–300 numbers are recoverable from that
> history. Small enough to import in an afternoon and nudge for ~S$6 total at
> utility rates — the reorder engine below was over-scoped for 15,000 but
> works identically at this size. The real gap is that nothing captures new
> numbers going forward; see `/internal/roadmap` for the fix.

Third, less urgent but it sets the ceiling: **what is your landed cost per
bottle?** It determines whether the wholesale ladder in `growth-config.ts` is
viable at all, and whether S$16 works for the 随身装.
